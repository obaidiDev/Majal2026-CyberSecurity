Your mission

Falcon Talent hired your red team to test one employee: a junior developer named Omar Nasser. You have no inside access — only what Omar has left lying around the open internet. Search him, open what you find, and reason out the password he reuses everywhere. Then prove you can log into his staff mailbox with it.

Nobody wrote his password down. You will have to build it from three separate facts scattered across his own pages — exactly how a real attacker turns a person's public life into a credential.

1 · Search Omar and pivot to the handle he reuses 2 · Clip the three facts that build his password 3 · Log in to the staff mail portal

Everything here is a self-contained simulation — a fake internet rendered inside this page. No real person, no real accounts, nothing leaves your browser. In the real world, logging into an account that isn't yours is a crime; the skill you are practising is defensive — seeing what your own footprint gives away.

Debrief — break your own chain

You needed every link: a reused handle to pivot, a public dog, a guessable recipe, a password that matched it. Flip on any one control and re-read the verdict — most of these are things Omar could have done in five minutes.

Password manager · unique random password

Every account gets a different 20-character random string. His public life no longer predicts anything.

✗ Chain broken: Rex2019! is now worthless — the password has no relationship to Rex, 2019, or anything you can find.
A different handle per site

The blog, the code host and the forum share no username, so nothing ties them to the same person.

✗ Chain broken: you never pivot from his blog to the forum rant, so the password recipe stays hidden.
Private photos · no personal details

The dog, the "gotcha day", the city — none of it is posted publicly.

✗ Chain broken: no Rex and no 2019 means there are no ingredients to assemble, recipe or not.
Passphrase + MFA on the mailbox

A long random passphrase no recipe produces, plus a second factor from his phone.

✗ Chain broken: even a leaked password fails — the recipe can't produce a passphrase, and MFA stops what's left.
Chain intact. With everything Omar left public, a stranger reasons out Rex2019! and walks into his mailbox. Flip a control above to cut a link.
